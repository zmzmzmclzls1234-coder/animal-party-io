Chapter 7: Photosynthesis & Cellular Respiration

Study notes - review before the quiz on Friday

1. Overview

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae and some bacteria use to turn light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It happens mainly in the chloroplasts of leaf cells, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll.

Overall equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2

2. The two stages

  • Light-dependent reactions (thylakoid membranes): light splits water, releasing O2; energy is stored in ATP and NADPH.
  • Calvin cycle (stroma): ATP and NADPH are used to fix CO2 into sugar. The enzyme RuBisCO captures the carbon.

3. Limiting factors

  • Light intensity - more light speeds it up until another factor limits it.
  • CO2 concentration
  • Temperature - enzymes work best around 25-35°C; too hot and they denature.

4. Cellular respiration (compare!)

Respiration releases the energy stored in glucose. It happens in the mitochondria: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP. Notice it is basically the reverse of photosynthesis.
